# Night artwork

The four matched character previews for My Village Shikha are used as the night cast: Bhumi in the main forest scene, a porcupine at the burrow, a masked palm civet on the crossing, and the established owl with amber highlights. They were created with the built-in ImageGen tool using the existing characters as material and identity references.

Character source prompts are retained in [NIGHT-CHARACTERS-PROMPTS.md](NIGHT-CHARACTERS-PROMPTS.md). Production relighting and rig-layer prompts follow.

Assets: `docs/assets/night-bhumi.png`, `night-porcupine.png`, `night-civet.png`, `night-civet-body.png`, `night-owl.png` and `night-forest.png`. The body layer is registered to the original civet so four individually clipped legs can use the existing scroll-driven gait.

## night-forest

Lighting-weather edit of this EXACT handmade cardboard forest panorama for My Village Shikha. Preserve the original composition, all tree trunks, rocks, cut-paper corrugation, bridge, distant Nepali hillside houses, stream, paths and terrain silhouettes at the same positions. Transform DAY into clear readable moonlit NIGHT. The sky becomes deep midnight indigo with a few tiny subtle paper stars and muted blue-gray paper clouds. Tree/rock faces turn deep blue-green with narrow silver moonlit highlights along cut edges, consistent light entering from upper RIGHT outside frame. Keep the foreground path visible, moss and cardboard grain legible, soft dark contact shadows. Distant village windows glow a little warm amber. Stream and waterfall catch pale blue-silver light. This is an enchanted Himalayan village forest, welcoming and calm, not horror or a pitch black silhouette. Keep all textures physically cut from matte crumpled corrugated cardboard with visible golden-kraft flutes, not photoreal foliage or smooth digital painting. DO NOT add a moon or sun: a separate interactive paper moon will be placed over the sky by the website. Do NOT add text, characters, animals, logos, interface or new objects. Preserve wide panoramic framing and original scene layout. No beach, sea or sand. The result is a production night background image for the same scene, not a concept board.
